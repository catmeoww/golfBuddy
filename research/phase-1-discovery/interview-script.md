# Discovery Interview Script

**Duration:** 30 minutes
**Facilitator:** Jordan (PM)  ·  **Notetaker:** Lin (UX)
**Goal:** understand how golfers practice, whether/how they use video, and what they wish was different — WITHOUT pitching our product.

## Guiding principles

1. **No leading questions.** Don't say "would you like an app that…" — ask about past behavior, not future wants.
2. **Follow the energy.** If they go somewhere interesting, chase it. The script is a safety net, not a rail.
3. **The 5 Whys.** When they say something interesting, ask "why" 2–3 times to get past the surface.
4. **Silence is fine.** Count to 5 before filling a pause; they often add the most useful thing right after.
5. **No demos, no mockups.** This is discovery. Concept testing is Phase 2.

---

## 0. Warm-up (3 min)

> "Thanks again for doing this. Before we get into golf, tell me a little about yourself — where you live, what you do for work, that kind of thing."

Purpose: put them at ease, calibrate their speech pace.

---

## 1. Their golf life (5 min)

> "How did you get into golf?"

> "How often are you playing or practicing these days?"

> "When you're practicing, is it usually at a range, at home, on the course? Walk me through a typical week."

> "Who do you usually go with?"

*Probes:* alone vs. friends ratio, how they decide, what's different between the two.

---

## 2. Last session — detailed walkthrough (10 min)

This is the meat. Specifics, not generalities.

> "Tell me about the last time you went to the driving range. As much detail as you can — when was it, who was there, what did you do first?"

*Probes (ask only what doesn't come up naturally):*
- How did you decide what to work on?
- How long were you there?
- Did you use your phone at all? (If yes: how?)
- Did you record anything?
  - If yes: why, what did you do with it after?
  - If no: have you ever? What stopped you this time?
- Did anything frustrate you during the session?
- Did you leave feeling like you'd learned something?

**Golden follow-ups:**
- "You said [X] — tell me more about that."
- "When you say [ambiguous word], what does that mean for you?"
- "Why did you [action]?"

---

## 3. Filming & feedback (7 min)

> "Have you ever tried to film your swing?"

If yes:
- What did you do with the video after?
- Did it help? How?
- What was frustrating about it?
- Did you ever share it with anyone? Who, and why?

If no:
- What's stopped you?
- If you could see yourself swing right now, what would you want to know?

> "How do you currently figure out what's working and what isn't in your swing?"

*Probes:* coaches, friends, feel, scores, YouTube, apps.

If they mention apps:
- Which ones? What did you like? What made you stop using it (if they did)?

> "Imagine you just hit a bad shot. What's the first thing you do?"

---

## 4. Wishlist — open-ended (3 min)

> "If you could wave a wand and change one thing about how you practice, what would it be?"

> "Is there anything about your golf game you wish you understood better?"

*Don't steer toward swing analysis. Let them take it where they take it.*

---

## 5. Close (2 min)

> "Anything I didn't ask about that I should have?"

> "If I have one follow-up question in a week, is it okay to email you?"

> "The gift card will land in your inbox by end of day tomorrow. Thanks so much — this was really helpful."

---

## Notetaker guide (for Lin)

- Capture direct quotes verbatim (use quotation marks)
- Mark emotional moments (laugh, frustration, hesitation) with `[emo: ...]`
- Flag anything surprising with `⚡`
- At end, write 3 bullets: "biggest takeaway / biggest surprise / biggest tension"

## Common pitfalls to avoid

- **"Would you use…"** questions — people say yes to be polite, data is worthless
- **Leading with features** — "so if you had a phase-detection view…" pollutes the sample
- **Summarizing back too much** — let them correct you but don't put words in their mouth
- **Chasing confirmation** — if 3 people in a row don't mention filming, that's data; don't steer the next one into it
